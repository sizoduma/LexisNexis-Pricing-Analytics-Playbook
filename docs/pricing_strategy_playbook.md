# Pricing strategy playbook

## 1. Pricing as a strategic lever

Pricing should not be treated as a tactical response to sales pressure. It should be a strategic engine for growth, value extraction, and competitive differentiation.

In a digital information and legal services company, price signals influence:
- customer perception of value
- product adoption and usage depth
- renewal confidence
- market competitiveness
- profitability quality

## 2. Value-based pricing principles

Value-based pricing is especially relevant for LexisNexis because customers are not buying generic data; they are buying productivity, risk reduction, legal compliance, and decision quality.

A good framework includes:
- segment value by customer type
- quantify outcomes (time saved, reduced risk, improved decisions)
- design packages according to intensity of use
- set prices based on perceived value and willingness-to-pay

## 3. Package design logic

For content-heavy, subscription-driven businesses, packaging should usually balance:

- access depth
- functionality and workflow support
- volume or usage caps
- support and service levels
- flexibility across business units and geographies

### Example package logic

- Basic: essential access for individual users
- Professional: broader research and workflow capabilities
- Enterprise: multi-user, compliance, reporting, and governance features
- Strategic: premium research, analytics, and bespoke support

## 4. Regional and market adaptation

The role explicitly mentions local relevance with global principles. This is crucial in international pricing.

Consider:
- local purchasing power
- customer maturity and product adoption
- regulatory context
- competitive intensity
- sales structure and channel mix

The best pricing frameworks are simple enough to be understood by sales teams, but flexible enough to adapt by market.

## 5. Discount governance

Discounts should support growth, not undermine value perception.

A practical policy should define:
- standard discount ranges by segment
- approval thresholds by customer size and region
- exceptions by business case
- evaluation of promotional effectiveness after launch

## 6. Campaign design and offer evaluation

Campaigns must be tested against business value, not just volume.

Metrics to monitor:
- acquisition conversion rate
- retention after promotion period
- average contract value uplift
- payback period
- cross-sell or upsell conversions

## 7. Customer value management

Pricing shapes customer lifetime value. It influences:
- retention
- expansion behavior
- risk of churn
- product mix and usage depth

This is why customer value management belongs inside pricing strategy.

## 8. Commercial narrative for interviews

A strong response to a pricing interview question is:

> I would build pricing strategies around customer value and measurable business outcomes rather than pure cost-plus logic. In a data-rich content environment, the real opportunity is to design packaging that reflects usage intensity, customer segment, and willingness to pay while ensuring guardrails around discounting and profitability. I’d use data and commercial reasoning together to create a model that is simple for sales teams, credible for finance, and measurable for growth.

## 9. Summary

The most valuable pricing strategy in this environment is one that is:

- value-based
- simple for customers and sales teams
- analytically grounded
- region-aware
- governance disciplined
- aligned with retention and expansion value
