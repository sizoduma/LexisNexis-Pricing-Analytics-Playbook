# Dashboard and KPI framework

## What pricing leaders need to track

A pricing dashboard should answer five operational questions:

1. Are we growing revenue without damaging margin?
2. Which segments are most price-sensitive?
3. Are promotions improving adoption or just discounting value?
4. Are customers renewing and expanding as expected?
5. Which markets need pricing recalibration?

## Recommended KPI suite

### Revenue and value metrics
- recurring revenue
- average selling price
- average revenue per account
- discount rate
- gross margin by product / segment

### Customer metrics
- retention rate
- churn rate
- renewal rate
- upsell/cross-sell conversion
- customer lifetime value

### Offer and campaign metrics
- conversion rate by promotion type
- incremental revenue generated
- promotional ROI
- churn impact after offer period

### Market and competitive metrics
- list price positioning versus competitors
- region-specific price acceptance
- market share movement
- discount variance by country or segment

## Dashboard design principles

- keep the layout simple and executive-friendly
- display trend and variance in the same view
- separate operational metrics from strategic decisions
- make segmentation drill-downs easy
- include clear threshold alerts for churn and discount risk

## Tool stack

Typical tooling in a role like this includes:
- Excel for scenario modeling
- Salesforce for commercial and deal data
- Tableau for dashboards
- Python for analysis and automation

## Example dashboard narrative

> A pricing dashboard should enable a manager to identify whether margin is being preserved while retention and pricing premium are being sustained. It should highlight which regions and segments are most responsive to offers and where discount discipline needs to be tightened.
