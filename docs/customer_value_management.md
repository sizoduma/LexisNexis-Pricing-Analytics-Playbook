# Customer value management and churn strategy

## Why this matters

In a subscription model, pricing and retention are tightly linked. If a customer does not see sustained value, pricing becomes a churn trigger rather than a growth signal.

For LexisNexis, the most relevant metrics include:
- churn rate by segment and region
- renewal probability
- expansion and cross-sell rate
- usage intensity
- customer health score
- upsell contribution by product line

## Core value management principles

### 1) Monitor value realization
Customers stay when the product delivers continuing business value. Pricing design should support that value realization.

### 2) Detect early warning signals
Common indicators:
- reduced usage frequency
- lower product penetration
- policy non-compliance or lower completion of workflows
- poor renewal sentiment from account teams

### 3) Link pricing to customer health
The role is not merely to monitor churn but to design offers and packaging that keep customers engaged and growing.

## A practical approach

### Segment customers by value and behavior
Create clusters such as:
- high usage / strategic accounts
- mid-market active users
- low usage / at-risk accounts
- cross-sell candidates

### Tie pricing actions to customer journeys
Examples:
- increase adoption through onboarding pricing or trial offer design
- reduce churn risk with premium support or usage-based access bundles
- expand account value with complementary content or advanced analytics packages

## Example objective metrics

- retention rate by plan and cohort
- churn rate by region and segment
- average revenue per account
- renewal uplift after package redesign
- campaign payback on marketing offers

## Practical interview angle

> I would connect pricing and customer value management by monitoring the relationship between product usage, renewal behavior, and customer segment. If a segment is showing weaker retention, I would review whether the issue is pricing architecture, packaging fit, or product value realization. That allows us to design targeted offers or package adjustments instead of broad discounting that weakens margin.

## Summary

Customer value management is not a separate discipline from pricing. It is the commercial outcome of a pricing model working effectively.
